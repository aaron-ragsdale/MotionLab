01 / Big picture

The movement pipeline

Camera frames→Landmark detector→Coordinates→Features→Classifier→Command

A pretrained detector estimates where body or hand points are in an image. MotionLab uses those points to compute movement signals or train a second model to recognize your own classes. These are two different machine learning tasks: the detector estimates locations; the classifier predicts labels.

Key insight

You do not need to train a camera-based detector from scratch to create a personalized gesture classifier.

02 / Camera & tracking

What is a landmarker?

A landmark is an estimated location of a meaningful point, such as a wrist or fingertip. MediaPipe predicts these locations from camera images; it does not measure joints directly.

Pose

33 body points. Useful for posture, arm movement, jumping and leaning.

Hands

21 points per hand. Useful for finger positions, pointing and hand gestures.

Both

Up to 75 points across one body and two hands. More detail, more processing.

Lite, Full and Heavy

These are different pose detector variants, not classifier algorithms. Lite prioritizes efficiency; Heavy generally needs more compute. Full is a useful starting point. GPU and CPU delegates describe how the detector runs, and performance varies by device.

03 / Live signal explorer

How is a landmark represented?

Image-normalized X and Y describe position relative to image width and height, usually near 0–1. Y increases downward. Z is an estimated relative depth, not a calibrated distance to the camera.

Coordinate spaceMeaning
Image normalizedRelative image coordinates; useful across resolutions.
Image pixelsX and Y scaled by image dimensions; Z scaled by width for visualization.
World estimatesPose model's estimated 3D coordinates in meters, relative to the body, not a calibrated room.
Shoulder-relativePose coordinates centered between shoulders and scaled by projected shoulder width.

MotionLab's current hand explorer supports normalized and pixel coordinates. Pose-specific visibility and joint-angle options are disabled for hands.

Key insight

A change in camera distance can change image coordinates even if your movement is identical. Normalization choices influence what a classifier learns.

04 / Live signal explorer

Position, velocity and acceleration

Position

Where the landmark is at a particular moment.

Velocity

Change in position divided by elapsed time: Δx / Δt.

Acceleration

Change in velocity divided by elapsed time: Δv / Δt.

Velocity describes direction and rate of motion. Speed is the magnitude of velocity. Acceleration describes how quickly velocity changes. Joint angles estimate the angle between two limb segments using three points.

MotionLab's pose angle explorer uses image-plane elbow and knee angles. These are not necessarily true anatomical 3D angles. Derivative units depend on the selected coordinate space, and timestamps matter when frames are irregular.

Try in MotionLab

Hold your wrist still, then move it quickly sideways and stop. Compare the position, velocity and acceleration graphs. Why is acceleration often noisier?

05 / Signal processing

Why do landmarks jitter?

Tracking is an estimate. Lighting, occlusion, camera resolution, model uncertainty and frame timing can cause small changes even when you are still. An exponential moving average (EMA) combines the newest observation with the previous filtered value:

filtered = α × raw + (1 − α) × previous

Orange dashed = raw, green = EMA. Synthetic data, not live measurements.

Lower alpha smooths more but can delay rapid movements. Alpha 1 uses the raw signal. Missing observations should be treated as gaps, not as zero positions.

Controller tradeoff

A smoother graph is not automatically a better controller. A fast punch may need lower latency than a stationary pointing gesture.

06 / Recording & dataset design

How do we collect useful training data?

A dataset is a collection of examples and their labels. Features are the numerical inputs the classifier receives. A class is a category you want it to recognize. A take is one separate recording attempt.

Record multiple independent takes per class, including a Neutral / Idle class. Vary positions, speeds and lighting within the range you expect during use. Avoid training one class in one corner of the camera and another class in a different corner unless location is intentionally part of the task.

The detailed trial recorder exports selected body and/or hand coordinates, timestamps and event markers. The unified Machine Learning Studio trains on selected or all available points and exports a portable classifier. It supports Pose, Hands or Both. Hand-containing classifier datasets use normalized image coordinates.

Key insight

Hundreds of nearly identical adjacent frames are not equivalent to hundreds of independent movements. Diversity of examples matters.

07 / Temporal recognition

Windowing and inference frequency

A static classifier sees a single landmark frame. A dynamic classifier sees a sequence of recent frames, called a temporal window. A sliding window advances as new frames arrive. Stride controls how often predictions update; it is independent of the amount of history used.

ExampleStarting window
Pointing or hand signOne frame
Punch or swipe150–300 ms
Tennis swing300–750 ms
Extended movement500–2000 ms

These ranges are starting points, not guaranteed optimal values. The unified studio resamples timestamped data to 30 Hz using the selected coordinates and landmark IDs. Changing the training window generally requires retraining. A longer window does not necessarily add its full duration to every prediction because windows overlap.

08 / Machine learning

What happens when we train?

Supervised classification uses labeled examples to learn how to associate feature vectors with classes. The unified studio supports five algorithms; they all receive the same feature format but learn differently.

AlgorithmHow it worksTradeoff
Random forestMany decision trees split examples using feature thresholds. Their outputs are averaged.Flexible nonlinear patterns; training and inference cost grow with tree complexity.
K-nearest neighborsFinds nearby saved examples in feature space and votes on their labels.Little training work; inference cost and exported size grow with the dataset.
Nearest centroidRepresents each class by its average feature vector and chooses the nearest.Compact and fast, but a single average may miss diverse examples of a class.
Linear SVMLearns maximum-margin linear boundaries in standardized feature space.Compact inference; cannot represent every nonlinear boundary.
RBF SVMUses a radial basis kernel to learn nonlinear maximum-margin boundaries.Training and inference cost depend on example count and support vectors.

All five can classify temporal feature vectors. SVM uses one-vs-rest classifiers and standardized features; C controls the penalty for training errors and gamma controls the locality of the RBF kernel. Sequence-specific approaches such as LSTMs, 1D CNNs and dynamic time warping are possible future additions, not currently implemented.

Try in MotionLab

Train your chosen algorithms using exactly the same takes. Compare their held-out scores, file sizes and behavior on unfamiliar movements.

09 / Evaluation

Accuracy is only one measure

A training set is used to fit a model; a held-out test set is used to evaluate it. MotionLab holds out separate takes rather than splitting adjacent frames randomly, reducing one source of leakage. Small held-out sets remain uncertain, and results may not generalize to new people.

A confusion matrix counts predicted classes against true classes. The unified studio displays a confusion matrix for the active trained model. Model scores from forest votes, KNN votes or centroid distances are not calibrated probabilities and should not be compared as if they were.

False activation

Triggers an action that was not intended.

Missed action

Fails to recognize an intended command.

Latency

Delay from movement to the game responding.

For controller design, test live behavior with neutral periods, transitions and movements outside the training set. Held-out accuracy alone does not measure these outcomes.

10 / Model deployment

How does a model become a controller?

The exported JSON stores the learned parameters, user-defined classes and input configuration. A compatible runtime can reconstruct the classifier and process landmarks using the same feature schema.

Prediction: point_left→Mapping: ArrowLeft→Game: move left
SettingPurpose
Confidence / score thresholdReject uncertain predictions.
ConfirmationRequire consistent predictions before acting.
Hold vs tapContinuous steering versus a one-time jump.
CooldownPrevent unwanted repeated actions.
Neutral / releaseStop issuing commands and release held inputs.

The unified studio trains, compares, tests and exports models. No second controller training step is required. Command mapping and a Chrome extension are planned, not yet installed or available from this page. A regular website cannot send unrestricted keyboard inputs to unrelated websites.

11 / Practical tradeoffs

Where does the computation happen?

MotionLab is hosted on GitHub Pages. The browser downloads JavaScript, WebAssembly and pretrained model assets, then processes camera frames locally. First startup can take longer; cached assets may speed up future starts, but permanent offline availability is not guaranteed.

Tracking FPS measures processed frames per second. Inference time measures detector processing, not total motion-to-command latency. Camera acquisition, buffering, filtering, classification and game rendering can all add delay.

No raw video is exported by the app. Landmark data can still be personal or sensitive. In particular, KNN and RBF SVM model exports can contain training feature vectors, so share exported datasets and models thoughtfully.

12 / Apply what you learned

Three experiments to try

01 · Make movement visible

Choose a fingertip or wrist. Move slowly, then rapidly. Compare position, velocity and acceleration. Change EMA alpha and look for lag.

02 · Design a responsive classifier

Record neutral, point left and point right as static classes. Train each algorithm on the same takes. Test from a different position. What changes?

03 · Explore temporal context

Train a punch or swipe with a short window, then collect a comparable dataset with a longer window. Compare live recognition speed and unwanted activations. Keep the detector, sample count and conditions as consistent as possible.
